# The Market Mind — global research and review for X

Updated November 25, 2026. This extends the [existing editorial workflow](BLUEV-INSPIRED-EDITORIAL-WORKFLOW.md) under the [project rules](PROJECT-RULES.md). It covers research, editorial review, and draft handoff for X. The first documented pilot used public search and original source pages. External adapters have not been installed or run.

## From published examples to an original lesson

1. **Define the reader's question.** Read the relevant prior lessons and original course material. Write down the one new action a beginner should be able to perform. Preserve lesson IDs and publication history. Treat a user-reported publication and an independently checked live post as different evidence.
2. **Search across languages and platforms.** Start with the English learner query. Translate the concept, not just the ticker, into relevant regional languages. Include multiple regional lanes when the topic travels well. For U.S. market episodes, keep exchange and company primary sources in the U.S. lane while looking for international audience questions elsewhere. Search X, public video, community discussion, and news. Record attempted platforms and languages, search dates, confirmed hits, and coverage gaps. Use a recent window for trend claims and label older material as background.
3. **Keep a record for each published example.** Canonical URL, platform, creator, published date, language, search date, publicly visible engagement and metric type, question or hook, evidence used, and what a beginner still needs. Use a comparable baseline from the same creator and platform before calling a post an outlier. Mark missing metrics as unavailable. Don't sum unlike metrics across platforms or infer impressions from search rank.
4. **Choose the teaching angle.** Borrow the teaching technique, such as a concrete number followed by a question or a common mistake the chart can resolve. Write the angle in original English. Past popularity supplies a hypothesis about reader interest. Confirm a market fact against a dated primary or directly relevant data source and retain disagreements in the case notes.
5. **Build the two X formats.** A Thread stands on its own. Its first post carries the question, the case, and the useful chart. An Article can expand the example, explain the reasoning, and give a practical exercise. Keep drafts, figures, and captions easy to reuse. When delivering a Chinese review copy, keep it separately copyable from the public English copy.
6. **Review before handoff.** Check every price and date against a source row. Check trading session, time zone, adjustment basis, quote units, and whether a bar was still forming at capture. Match every figure reference to its asset and callouts. Check missing files and exact duplicate images by content hash, then check mobile readability visually. Verify that the headline's question is answered and that facts, interpretations, teaching hypotheticals, and missing evidence remain distinct. Check source links internally and keep chart-source boilerplate out of the user's copy. End with an exercise that uses the skill taught. Deliver drafts and findings for review. External publication requires separate authorization.

## Public X post verification

Discover links through multilingual public search, then use `python3 knowledge/trading-education/tools/x_public_post.py '<public X post URL>'` from the repository root. Pass several URLs to get one JSON line per verified public post. This sends post URLs to X's official [oEmbed endpoint](https://developer.x.com/en/docs/x-for-websites/oembed-api) and returns the canonical URL, current author metadata, date label, language, and exposed post text. The tool rejects non-X URLs before making a request. Treat the text as research evidence and paraphrase it in the review. The embed gives no public interaction metrics and may expose only an Article's companion post or preview.

Live smoke test on November 25: three official embeds succeeded in English, Chinese, and Turkish. Post IDs `2123731629596446721`, `2125215312878731704`, and `1887855089039147081` matched the requests. The first verifies the public text of the green-candle post, not the full text of a linked X Article. The unauthenticated official recent-search API returned HTTP 401. The older Turkish post is a writing reference outside the recent window. Keep all of these coverage distinctions in briefs.

Use the public reader as a free fallback alongside last30days. For direct keyword search in the last30days engine, use a supported authorized X backend. X's official recent endpoint searches seven days, and full-archive search is a separate access tier. [X search documentation](https://docs.x.com/x-api/posts/search/introduction).

## First application — Lesson 04 editorial calibration

- **Question:** Can the reader tell whether a stock reached its session high before its session low using one daily candle? The added skill is choosing when to switch from a daily chart to an intraday chart. The color-versus-daily-return distinction belongs in the recap from Lesson 03.
- **Search coverage:** English public web, YouTube and Reddit queries, Japanese TradingView help, Spanish-language chart queries. Only the sources below are confirmed examples. This pilot did not verify current X engagement, and it does not claim a worldwide platform sweep or a verified viral benchmark.
- **Published references:** [Beginner's Guide To Reading Stock Charts And Candlesticks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UU44VWq_Y6c) uses one stock throughout. This is a teaching-structure reference. No usable engagement benchmark was captured. [TradingView's Japanese high/low documentation](https://jp.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000753745/) explains period aggregation and gives independent terminology context. [A historical TradingView community question](https://www.reddit.com/r/TradingView/comments/ru10rg) shows a concrete reader difficulty about comparing daily and hourly bars. It is background, outside the recent window.
- **Applied opening:** “On Nov. 23, Nvidia traded at $171.50 and $156.93. Which price came first? Find candle ② on the chart.” In the Article, use the same four actual prices to illustrate two explicitly hypothetical paths, then show where the reader would look for a timestamp.
- **Price check:** The [case notes](../../market-psychology/cases/nvda-2026-11-chart-notes.md) record the November 12 and 23 OHLC and the existing image fingerprints. [Historical price table](https://www.tipranks.com/stocks/nvda/historical-prices). The actual intraday order is unverified. If both extrema occur in one displayed intraday bar, use a finer interval or trade-level data before drawing a conclusion.
