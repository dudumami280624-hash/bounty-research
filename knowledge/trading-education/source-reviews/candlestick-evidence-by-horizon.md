# Candlestick evidence by horizon

Reviewed November 25, 2026. This source review adds research context for future lessons and for the weekly trend comparison. The results below are reported by their authors. They have not been independently replicated for The Market Mind.

## Book and method

The [publisher catalog for Steve Nison's Beyond Candlesticks](https://www.wiley-vch.de/en/areas-interest/finance-economics-law/beyond-candlesticks-978-0-471-00720-3) documents chapters on candle patterns and their broader technical context, followed by other chart types and moving averages. [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Beyond_Candlesticks.html?id=EirI-2m3tj4C) offers a limited preview. No accessible sample page was verified for the exact hammer or shooting-star rule in this pass. Do not assign a page citation or specific numerical definition to Nison on this evidence. See the [edition and lesson-fit note](nison-beyond-candlesticks-scope.md).

## Empirical results and what they measure

| Study | Test and sample | What the authors reported | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Jamaloodeen, Heinz and Pollacia (2018)](https://scholars.fhsu.edu/jiibr/vol5/iss1/5/) | S&P 500 daily candles. Hammer and shooting star compared with all candles, using separate price-extreme and closing-price criteria over the next 5, 7 and 10 trading days. See the [full paper, methodology and Tables 3–8](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/214528289.pdf). | The tested highs/lows gave stronger results than the closing-price criteria. Closing-price results alone offered little forecasting reliability. | A short-window extreme test differs from a future return or long-term trend test. |
| [Marshall, Young and Rose (2006)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378426605002116) | Candlestick trading rules on DJIA stocks, assessed with a bootstrap approach. | The tested strategies did not add value in their sample. | Result applies to the paper's rules, universe, and testing design. |
| [Lu, Chen and Hsu (2015)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426615002678) | DJIA components, three competing trend definitions and different holding rules. | Eight three-day reversal patterns were profitable under one tested holding rule after the authors accounted for data snooping and imposed a 0.5% transaction cost. The alternative holding rule did not yield profitable patterns. | A positive result for these particular patterns and implementation does not establish a long-horizon effect for hammer and shooting star. |
| [Lu and Shiu (2012)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1016/j.rfe.2012.02.001) | Two-day candlestick rules applied to the Taiwan stock market, with an opposite-pattern exit in the tested strategy. | The authors found three bullish reversal patterns profitable in their test. | A useful non-US comparison. The pattern, holding rule, and market must be carried together when citing the result. |
| [Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X11002613) | Return persistence over one to twelve months across 58 liquid equity-index, FX, commodity, and bond futures. | The authors found return persistence over those horizons, with partial reversal at longer horizons. | A long-horizon benchmark concept. It does not test candlesticks or individual-stock hammer and shooting-star signals. |

## Application to the series

Candlesticks condense a trading period into an open, high, low, and close and make the range, closing location, and shape easy to compare on a chart. Treat those as descriptive strengths. Evidence for a future outcome requires a separate test against the right benchmark.

For a future US-stock comparison, use actual weekly and monthly OHLC for a documented multi-stock universe. Fix the pattern and preceding-trend definitions before reviewing outcomes. Compare equivalent trend contexts with and without the pattern. Report forward close-to-close returns and maximum favorable/adverse excursions separately at predeclared horizons, for example 13, 26, and 52 weeks. Record split handling, sample counts, delistings where applicable, overlapping signals, and uncertainty. Use a held-out period and retain failures and counterexamples alongside visually clear examples. This is a proposed test design. No new market backtest or chart audit was run for this note.
