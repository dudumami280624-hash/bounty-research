## Independent literature
- Jamaloodeen et al. (2018), DOI 10.58809/OEJO4473, https://scholars.fhsu.edu/jiibr/vol5/iss1/5/ : over six decades of S&P 500 data; results differ between close-based and extreme-price criteria.
- Marshall et al. (2006), DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2005.08.001: tested DJIA stock strategies did not demonstrate investment value.
- Caginalp and Laurent (1998), Applied Mathematical Finance 5, 181–205: positive results for tested three-day patterns, not direct validation of single-candle patterns.

## Original teaching card
A long wick records a price excursion, not a promise about tomorrow. A hammer near support or a shooting star near resistance can identify a level worth watching. Define confirmation before entering and distinguish a respected price level from a profitable trade.

Exercise (hypothetical): a hammer closes at 102 with a low of 94. A trader enters next day at 104 and exits five sessions later at 103, with no intervening low below 94. The support test succeeds under that definition, while the trade loses 1/104 = 0.96% before costs. These are distinct outcome measures.

Disposition: preserve context and confirmation concepts. Comparative win-rate and volume claims remain open, not declared false. Next: collect actual dated OHLC/volume, matched counterexamples, and preregister outcome criteria. No independent backtest or chart-pixel verification claimed.
