# Chapter 01 Revision — The Market Is a Conversation

## Revised claim

Public policy events can be followed by short-horizon market-return differences. The current evidence does not establish that narrative change caused those returns through order flow.

## Evidence retained

- FOMC event and matched non-event comparison;
- 74 matched pairs, 73 usable pairs;
- SEC MIDAS SPY 2022 Q4 market-activity metrics;
- CPI, unemployment and federal-funds controls;
- SEC 8-K filing-date control for major constituents;
- Bootstrap and placebo tests.

## Current results

- The controlled 5-day event effect remains negative at approximately -0.75%.
- The Bootstrap 95% interval includes zero: approximately -1.67% to +0.10%.
- The placebo test also produces a negative difference.
- Therefore the event association is not stable enough to support a causal claim.

## What the evidence supports

The evidence supports only this narrower statement:

> Policy announcement days were associated with short-horizon return differences in the tested sample.

It does not support:

> Narrative change caused the price move through order flow.

## Limitations

- The event sample is limited by available daily index history.
- SEC MIDAS alignment is complete only for the 2022 Q4 SPY slice.
- The 8-K control covers six major constituents, not every index constituent.
- The control model uses filing counts, not earnings surprise magnitude or announcement timestamp.
- The placebo result indicates possible period or matching-rule effects.
- Bid-ask and full order-level data are not available in the free stack.

## Grill status

- Status: PRELIMINARY_CAUSAL_COMPARISON
- Causal conclusion: NOT ESTABLISHED
- Publication readiness: BLOCKED for a causal claim
- Allowed wording: association, event-window difference, preliminary evidence
- Prohibited wording: caused, proves, reliable causal mechanism, VERIFIED
