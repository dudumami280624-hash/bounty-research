# Loss aversion

**Definition.** In prospect theory, outcomes are evaluated relative to a reference point. A loss can carry greater psychological weight than an equal-sized gain.

**Observable behavior.** When two matched choices are framed around the same reference point, a person may respond more strongly to the prospect of losing $100 than to gaining $100. Terms, probabilities, and available alternatives matter when interpreting that response.

**U.S. market example.** Illustrative brokerage scenario. An investor considers changes that would add or subtract $100 in comparable positions. They spend far more time avoiding the downside scenario and describe the possibility of realizing the loss as the main reason for their decision. The scenario illustrates the mechanism and does not identify it in a real account.

**Counterexample.** A household that needs cash for rent may rationally attach greater importance to a $100 loss. Liquidity needs, taxes, and exposure limits can also explain asymmetric choices.

**Decision guardrail.** State the reference point, write the gain and loss cases side by side, and log any cash or risk constraints separately. This makes it easier to see what is driving the comparison.

**Evidence status.** Reference-dependent valuation is grounded in prospect theory. This card distinguishes it from the disposition effect, which describes a realization pattern. See [Kahneman and Tversky, 1979](https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185).
