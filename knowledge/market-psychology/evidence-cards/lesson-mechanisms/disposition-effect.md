# Disposition effect

**Definition.** A tendency to realize gains more readily than losses, relative to the opportunities to realize each. It is an observed trading pattern. A chart or a single sale cannot identify its motive.

**Observable behavior.** An investor repeatedly sells winners while keeping comparable losers, then cites getting back to even as a reason to hold. The relevant comparison counts available positions with unrealized gains or losses, as well as completed sales.

**U.S. market example.** Odean used transaction records from 10,000 discount-brokerage accounts to compare when investors realized winners and losers. The sample favored realizing gains, with tax-related loss selling more evident in December.

**Counterexample.** A sale of a winner can reflect a documented rebalancing rule. Holding an unrealized loss while fundamentals remain consistent with the original thesis can also have a reason independent of the purchase price.

**Decision guardrail.** Write down the current thesis and its disconfirming evidence, then review the cost basis separately for taxes or accounting. Ask whether the same evidence would produce the same decision if the gain/loss badge were hidden.

**Evidence status.** Observational support in the brokerage sample. The guardrail is an educational adaptation. See [Odean, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00072) and [existing Evidence Card 003](../CARD-003-DISPOSITION-STOPLOSS.md).
