# Anchoring

**Definition.** An initial number can exert an outsized influence on a later judgment, even after the person receives other relevant information.

**Observable behavior.** An investor repeatedly resets a valuation near a remembered purchase price or prior high. The stated thesis changes, but the estimate stays close to the original number without a fresh derivation.

**U.S. market example.** Classroom application using the [November 23 NVDA case](../../cases/nvda-2026-11-23-red-candle.md). After seeing the $171.50 intraday high in a headline, a reader treats that number as a fair-value benchmark for $165.05. The two prices are verified. The reader's judgment is an illustration.

**Counterexample.** The previous close is a legitimate starting point for calculating a daily return. A recent transaction price can also serve as a reference when its relevance to the question is established.

**Decision guardrail.** Name the question before selecting the number. When estimating value, build a fresh range from relevant inputs and then compare it with the remembered price.

**Evidence status.** The mechanism is grounded in experimental judgment research. Its application to this specific NVDA reader is hypothetical. See [Tversky and Kahneman, 1974](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124).
