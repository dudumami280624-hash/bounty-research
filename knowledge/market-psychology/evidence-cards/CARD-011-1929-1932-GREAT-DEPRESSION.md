# CARD-011 — 1929–1932 Great Depression

- Status: REVIEWED, chapter use pending
- Core claim: A price break becomes systemic when leverage, banking fragility, deflation and policy error reinforce one another.
- Model mapping: Balance/Imbalance; Leverage Changes the Geometry.
- US evidence: NBER reviews link the 1929 crash, weak banking structure and subsequent bank panics to the depth of the Depression.
- Primary sources:
  - https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w6015/w6015.pdf
  - https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15584/w15584.pdf
- Counterexample: Not every large equity decline becomes a decade-long depression; later crises had different monetary and institutional responses.
- Invalidation: The claim fails if similar leverage and banking stress repeatedly produce only a short market correction under comparable policy conditions.
- Adaptation: This is an original systemic-risk model, not a claim that Chanlun predicts the Depression.
- Missing: harmonized price, bank-failure and money-supply series; chapter-specific chart.
