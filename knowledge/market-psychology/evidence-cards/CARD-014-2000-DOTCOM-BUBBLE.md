# CARD-014 — 2000 Dot-Com Bubble

- Status: REVIEWED, chapter use pending
- Core claim: Narrative expansion can detach valuation from operating evidence, but the detachment is only visible after defining the evidence beforehand.
- Model mapping: Market as Conversation; Exhaustion and Divergence.
- US evidence: NBER research documents the rapid rise into 2000, unprecedented valuation ratios and internet-sector concentration.
- Primary sources:
  - https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c0124/c0124.pdf
  - https://www.nber.org/papers/w8630
  - https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8221/w8221.pdf
- Counterexample: High valuation alone does not identify the timing of a crash; some expensive companies continue to compound.
- Invalidation: The claim fails if pre-specified valuation, earnings and narrative measures do not distinguish the bubble sample from comparable growth periods.
- Adaptation: Do not equate “new technology” with irrationality.
- Missing: pre-registered valuation and earnings thresholds.
