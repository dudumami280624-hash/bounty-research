# Reader Pain-Point and Self-Recognition

## Purpose

Make The Market Mind content emotionally recognizable to experienced investors. The reader should see a real decision they have made, avoided, rationalized or regretted, then want to keep reading.

## Pain-point map

Before writing, choose one primary pain:

- Entered after the move and called it confirmation.
- Held a losing position because selling would make the loss real.
- Sold a winner too early and watched it continue higher.
- Knew the setup but froze when it appeared.
- Added leverage to recover a previous loss.
- Confused a good company with a good price.
- Mistook a compelling story for evidence.
- Kept changing systems after a short losing streak.
- Blamed the market instead of the decision process.
- Had a rule, then negotiated with it in real time.

## Recognition test

A draft passes only if:

1. It opens with a concrete behavior, not a theory.
2. The reader can say: "I have done this."
3. The emotional conflict appears before the explanation.
4. The post names the rationalization the reader used.
5. The ending changes how the reader sees their own decision.

## X format lock

- Standard post: 240–260 characters in the English publication text.
- One post contains one pain, one turn, one insight.
- If the idea needs more space, split into a numbered thread.
- Chinese review text is separate and does not count toward the English limit.

## Evidence and ethics

- Do not invent a reader's biography or diagnose them.
- Use "you may have" or a concrete universal behavior, not false certainty about the reader's mind.
- Connect the pain to a documented psychological mechanism and mark the evidence status.
- Do not use shame, fear or promises of profit to force engagement.

## Workflow

Source material → pain-point selection → concrete scene → emotional turn → psychological mechanism → US-market adaptation → bilingual review → grill-with-docs → publication-quality-control.
