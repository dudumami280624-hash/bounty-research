# Angkor Cinematic Documentary

Use this skill when the user asks for an Angkor series episode, a shooting plan, a storyboard or a review of an Angkor cut. This line is pure appreciation, not a race for likes, and it is separate from the fruit money line.

## Lock the landmark first

- Check the number table before starting any episode. Match the episode number to the real landmark, and never guess the number from a production note.

- Lock one landmark per episode. Never mix two temples into one episode, and never present a feature from another temple as if it belonged to this one.

- Learn the real building first from real photos, real travel notes and real blogger videos. Never copy another person's AI finished work as if it were your own, and never present a learned preparation as a completed study.

- Write only directions and relationships in prompts and storyboards. Never write a distance, a height, a kilometre number or a level number that has not been verified by real shooting evidence.

## Report before shooting

- Report the wow core, the light, the camera movement, the stone colour, the light colour and the shadow colour for this episode, and state how each differs from the previous episode, before generating anything.

- Make the wow core unique and unmatched, not merely good looking. A generic corridor, courtyard or walking monk is not a wow core.

- Follow the first 2 second rule. At second 0 the wow element is already in the picture, with no black fade and no slow fog reveal. At second 1 the camera is already moving. At second 2 the question is still unanswered.

- Give each episode an enter, discover, climax and afterglow visual journey, so the episode feels complete without narration.

- Give each episode a different overall feeling where the series plan calls for it, such as awe, mystery, time, nature or civilisation, and never repeat the same light and camera trick for dozens of episodes.

## Shoot for ancient feeling

- Keep the ancient palette. Use warm grey stone, dark green moss, golden light and blue grey shadow. Avoid bright blue sky, bright green forest, clean white new stone and bright HDR colour.

- Use light as the main tool, not post production sharpening. The user handles high definition post work. Your delivery focus is shooting method, light, storyboard and spirit.

- Start with a question in the first look and give the answer only at the end where the episode plan calls for it. A beautiful picture alone is not enough.

- Chain the episodes with picture hooks only. Let the head of an episode echo the tail of the previous episode, and let the tail point to the next landmark, without narration or text.

## Keep the series format

- Keep 9 to 16 vertical, real cinematic photography, real architecture and natural light. Keep the cut quiet, with no assistant narration and no Chinese text in the video, so the user can add music later.

- Let duration follow the content. Add time when an episode has more to explain, and shorten it when it has less. Never force 30 seconds, 40 seconds or 90 seconds, and never cut an explanation short just to hit a fixed length.

- Accept slight drift where the user accepts it. Do not burn tokens repeatedly only for perfect consistency, and do not claim a cut is perfect when known limits remain, such as 720p, AI suggested carving or slight tower drift.

- When the user says the cut is acceptable, treat that episode as sealed and do not reopen it for rework without a new user decision.

## Teach while making

- Teach the user the shooting method behind each choice, not only the finished cut. Explain what can be borrowed, why it works, how it applies to the next episode and what must not be copied.

- Check risk before posting, naming and formatting, not only after posting. Remind the user to open AI generation disclosure on every platform and to choose English, and to check the daily tag choice at posting time rather than using a fixed list.
