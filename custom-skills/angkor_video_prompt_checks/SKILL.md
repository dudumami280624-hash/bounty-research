# Angkor video prompt checks

## Write counter wording into the first prompt version

- Write the exact counter wording for each generator default into the first prompt version, and do not wait for a failed generation to add it.

- Describe a low brick shrine as a low horizontal row that fills the middle third of the frame when the real temple is low, and do not rely on the word tower alone.

- Describe a tower top as a stepped top and state that the central tower is only slightly higher with a small height difference, and do not rely on the word highest alone.

- Describe a carving as a shallow relief with brick seams passing through the figure and state that it is not an independent statue.

- Use a slow push in for a giant close up, and do not use a pull back for that shot.

- Write male features explicitly when a deity is male, including broad shoulders and a square jaw and a moustache and a flat chest and a muscular torso.

- Write the erosion degree into the first prompt version, including severe erosion or worn surfaces when the real site is heavily worn, and do not add it only after a generation looks too perfect.

- Read the master charter in the learning warehouse before you start a new episode, and use this skill for the exact wording that the charter states only in general terms.

## Verify direction, hook and narration from more than one source

- Do not infer a compass direction from the order of items in one source list, and require a plan or a map plus at least one independent source before you assign a direction.

- Record each source conflict in the episode file, and choose the shot design that avoids an unverified detail when the sources still disagree.

- Check the identity and the layout and the count and the carving type and the real condition of the site before you write prompts, and do not generate from one text description alone.

- Keep a real photograph of the site beside each prompt while you write it, and change the prompt when the photograph contradicts a detail in the text.

- Verify every claim in the opening hook and the narration against the episode evidence file before you draft it, and do not present a legend or a nickname story as a confirmed fact.

- Treat a name legend as a name legend only, and do not turn it into a use claim about what the building was for.

- Build the opening as one giant close up that fills the vertical frame at 0 seconds with the camera already moving at 1 second and one past present contrast question at 2 seconds with no answer and no year.

- Ask only one question in the hook, and do not answer it in the same line or add a second question.

- Check the hook against the four retention standards the user supplied from the TikTok official article, namely recognisable at once and a second layer fact worth saving and an unexpected insight and close to the wish to walk in the place.

- Keep the narration to what the finished frames show, and do not write a line about a place or a state that belongs to another episode.

## Count the finished segment frame by frame

- Count every countable element in the finished frames, including towers and doors and trees and heads, and do not judge a segment only by whether it looks right.

- Write a multi headed animal by head positions only, using left and middle and right, and write the heads without describing whole animal bodies.

- Do not write one animal with three heads, because the generator can give the extra heads to the figure standing on the animal.

- Do not write a bare number for heads, because the generator can add one extra head, and write the positions instead.

- Check the opening frame for one subject that fills the vertical frame with clear texture and restrained warm side light, and reject a frame where the main subject is cropped out or lost in a wide view.

- Write 720 by 1280 vertical format into every segment prompt, and check each finished segment is 720 by 1280 before you assemble the episode.

## Judge only from a complete check

- Wait for the full backend before you judge a hook, and read total plays and average watch time and completion rate and the 0 to 2 second stop note together.

- Do not call a hook a win from the like rate alone, and do not call it a loss from one partial number alone.

- Say the verdict is not yet made when any of those numbers is still missing, and retract the earlier verdict at once when the full numbers contradict it.

- Check every frame of a re-edited episode in one pass before you deliver it, and do not split the check across passes or report a partial count as a full check.

- Report every defect you find as a defect to fix, and do not call a found defect a minor detail or a background detail or leave it unfixed.

- Fix every defect you find before you deliver, and do not deliver a cut that still holds a defect you already named.

## Keep the whole subject in frame for the whole shot

- Write the retained range into the first prompt version for a close up, and name the features that must stay in frame for the whole shot, such as both eyes and nose and mouth and chin and forehead for a face, or head and ears and eyes and trunk for an elephant.

- End a push in on the whole subject, and reject a segment whose final frame crops the face or head even when its opening frame is complete.

- Check the start frame and a middle frame and the final frame of every segment, and do not judge a close up only by its opening frame.

- Start an opening move on the identifying feature, such as the head or the face, and do not start on a body or a detail that only reveals the subject after several seconds.

- When the subject is dead or dry, write into the first prompt version that the background holds no green leaves, and name the dry state of the subject itself.

## Change the shot after a repeated failure

- Change the shot when the same failure happens twice, and use a different angle or a different framing or a simpler movement or a close detail of a real component.

- Fix only the prompt words that caused the failure, and do not rebuild the whole episode to repair one segment.

- Stop and ask the user when a correction changes a historical fact or a visual identity, and do not guess a new fact to keep generating.

## Available connectors

- Use muse.connector_search to find other connectors and muse.skill_search to find related skills.

- If nothing here fits, look on the web for a maintained API or MCP server and set it up with Custom Connectors and MCPs, or build a custom connector.

## Alternatives for better results

- Ask the user to choose the visual core before you write prompts when several cores are possible, and keep the chosen core visible in every segment.

- Use a real component close up as the opening giant when no whole building fits the vertical frame, and do not invent a statue or a tower to fill the frame.

- Leave the next episode number out of a direction unless the user asked for it, and label a future episode clearly when you must mention it.
